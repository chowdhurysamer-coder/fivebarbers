The Shop

The chairs have changed.
The idea hasn't.

The neon went up on Walt Whitman Road in 1955, back when a shave and a haircut were how a man kept his appointments with the world. Seventy years later, the pole still spins, the clippers still hum, and the coffee's still on.

No stylists, no upsell, no forty-minute "consultation." Just barbers — the kind who learned the trade with a straight razor and a strop, and who'll give you exactly the cut you asked for. Talk if you feel like talking. Read the paper if you don't. Either way you're out of the chair looking sharp.

— and if you hear piano coming from the back, that's just Sergio on his break.

  • 01

    Walk-ins only, always

    No booking apps, no waitlists. The wait is short and the chairs turn quick — that's how it's worked since Eisenhower.

  • 02

    Every head, every age

    First haircuts, school pictures, job interviews, weddings. Kids and seniors get a discount price, and everyone gets the same care.

  • 03

    Clean shop, fair price

    A proper haircut for $16 and a hot towel shave for $20. Spotless stations, fresh towels, plenty of parking out back.

The house specialty

The Hot Towel Shave

Twenty minutes. Steamed towels, hot lather, a straight razor with a fresh edge, and a barber who's done this a few thousand times. It ends with a cold towel and a slap of tonic, and it fixes more than your face.

Ask for it before a wedding, after a rough week, or for no reason at all. It's $20 — the best twenty dollars on Route 110.
